## Research Findings

M-IRT (Metamorphic Item Response Theory) is a reference-free, contamination-resilient LLM evaluation framework that inverts the frozen-held-out-test-set axiom by combining (a) on-the-fly generated Metamorphic Item Clusters (MICs) of 5 variants each (seed + paraphrase + negation + contrapositive + distractor), (b) Samejima's Graded Response Model (GRM, K=4) fit via Bock-Aitkin marginal MLE over cluster-level consistency grades (0–3), and (c) a simulated contamination protocol that injects (q, a*) into one cluster member only and measures whether static accuracy rises while M-IRT's latent ability estimate does not [1, 7, 8]. Unlike ATLAS [2] (3PL on a fixed calibrated bank of 3000+ LLMs; MAE 0.157 on HellaSwag with 41 items) and PSN-IRT [3] (4PL neural calibration on 41,871 items across 11 benchmarks), M-IRT requires no ground-truth answer key because grades are computed from the model's own outputs across an MIC's variants. Unlike tinyBenchmarks [4] (IR++ predicts MMLU accuracy within 1.9% MAE using 100 items, but IRT is used only for selection, not the evaluation itself) and the GRM-for-LLM-Judges paper [6] (GRM on Likert ratings to measure judge reliability, CV<0.1 consistency threshold, ρ>0.7 marginal reliability), M-IRT's GRM is fit on the *model being evaluated*, not the judge, and the response is cluster-level consistency rather than a quality rating. The four-MR set (paraphrase, negation, contrapositive, distractor) is grounded in two precedents: LGMT [10] derives 20 FOL-grounded MRs from De Morgan / contraposition / quantifier transformations and shows models are most sensitive to symbol- and conclusion-level variations; LLMORPH [17] catalogues 191 NLP MRs, identifies MRs 9/142/154 as high-failure-low-FP, and runs 561,267 tests with ~60% true-positive rate. M-IRT avoids the LLMORPH semantic-drift failure mode by using template-level grammar substitution at the MIC-generator level rather than neural paraphrasing. The four reasoning-domain sub-generators mirror PrOntoQA [11] (PCFG over a typed ontology, parametric difficulty d ∈ {2,4,6,8}, ground truth derivable from the generator itself), GSM8K [12] / MAWPS [13] (arithmetic word problems with distractor invariance), FOLIO [15] (FOL-annotated NL pairs, machine-verified correctness), and LogicBench [14] (25 single-rule syllogistic patterns, negation-sensitive). Concrete estimator settings: Samejima GRM with K=4, Bock-Aitkin EM with MML, 41-node Gauss-Hermite quadrature on θ ∈ [-4, 4], convergence max |Δα|, |Δβ_k| < 1e-4 across all items or 500 iterations, RMSEA ≤ 0.08 acceptable fit, software girth (Python) or mirt (R via rpy2), 80–150 clusters recommended given n_models ≤ 10. Contamination protocol: inject a* into the seed q's prompt only, leave 4 MR variants clean; negative control injects a random string; success criterion is Δ_static_acc > 0.1 AND Δ_θ_M-IRT < 0.05 within posterior SE. Reviewer-anticipated rebuttals (imitation without reasoning, n_models < 10, multi-injection-type robustness, generator-as-oracle, multi-dimensional ability) are addressed in §8 of the report via impossible-premise negative controls, Bayesian fallback priors from [6], three-level injection (semantic/information/label), and per-domain GRM fitting. The 16 cited sources include the four positioning anchors (HELM, ATLAS, PSN-IRT, tinyBenchmarks, GRM-for-Judges), the contamination methodology (Xu survey, Sainz TS-Guessing), the metamorphic precedents (LGMT, LLMORPH, Chen survey), the GRM citation (Samejima 1969), and the four reasoning benchmarks (PrOntoQA, GSM8K, MAWPS, LogicBench, FOLIO). ## Sources

[1] [Holistic Evaluation of Language Models (HELM)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110) — Foundational multi-metric LLM evaluation paper by Percy Liang et al. (Stanford). Frames the contamination problem as an externally reported attribute and explicitly acknowledges that 'we have uneven and usually limited information on the training procedure for these models' — motivating M-IRT's reference-free stance.

[2] [Adaptive Testing for LLM Evaluation: A Psychometric Alternative to Static Benchmarks (ATLAS)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04689) — ICML 2026 Spotlight. 3PL IRT with Fisher-information-guided item selection on a fixed calibrated bank of 3000+ LLMs across 5 benchmarks. MAE 0.157 on HellaSwag using 41 items; 90% item reduction. Uses 3PL p_i(θ) = c_i + (1−c_i)/(1+exp(−a_i(θ−b_i))). Positioned against M-IRT: requires calibrated bank, dichotomous responses.

[3] [Lost in Benchmarks? Rethinking Large Language Model Benchmarking with Item Response Theory (PSN-IRT)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15055) — AAAI 2026 Oral. Pseudo-Siamese Network for IRT using 4PL neural calibration. Trained end-to-end on 11 benchmarks comprising 41,871 items. Reports that PSN-IRT-selected items yield stronger alignment with human preference than raw items. Positioned against M-IRT: requires 4PL with feasibility parameter, fixed item bank, large-scale binary response matrix.

[4] [tinyBenchmarks: evaluating LLMs with fewer examples](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14992) — ICML 2024. Uses IRT (Rasch/2PL and neural) for item *selection* only — IRT++ predicts MMLU accuracy within 1.9% MAE using 100 of 14K items. Positioned against M-IRT: IRT is a regression prior, evaluation still requires ground-truth accuracy; M-IRT eliminates ground truth.

[5] [Estimation of latent ability using a response pattern of graded scores (Samejima 1969)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03372177) — Canonical Samejima GRM paper (Psychometrika 34(S1):1–97). Defines the polytomous IRT model with ordered category boundaries β_k, the foundation of M-IRT's response model.

[6] [Diagnosing the Reliability of LLM-as-a-Judge via Item Response Theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00521) — ICML 2026. Applies Samejima GRM to LLM-judge reliability. Provides the canonical likelihood P(Y≥k|θ) = 1/(1+exp(−α(θ−β_k))), Bayesian priors (θ~N(0,1), α~LogNormal(0,0.5), β_k~N(0,1) with ordering), CV<0.1 consistency threshold, ρ>0.7 marginal reliability, and 2PL collapse when K=2. Source of M-IRT's estimator settings and priors.

[7] [Benchmark Data Contamination of Large Language Models: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04244) — Survey defining the four-level contamination taxonomy (semantic/information/data/label) and the matching-based vs comparison-based detection dichotomy. Source of M-IRT's contamination protocol: label-level injection targets the most damaging form of contamination.

[8] [Investigating Data Contamination in Modern Benchmarks for Large Language Models (TS-Guessing)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09783) — NAACL 2024. Defines the TS-Guessing protocol: mask a wrong multiple-choice option or an unlikely word, check whether the model fills the gap verbatim. Reports ChatGPT 52% and GPT-4 57% exact-match rate on MMLU. The masking-and-guessing paradigm is generalised to M-IRT's cluster-consistency detection.

[10] [LGMT: Logic-Grounded Metamorphic Testing for Evaluating the Reasoning Reliability of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23965) — Knowledge-Based Systems 2026. Derives 20 FOL-grounded MRs from De Morgan / contraposition / quantifier transformations, avoiding the semantic-drift failure of lexical paraphrasing. Constructs 76,298 candidate metamorphic groups; evaluates 3,091 sampled across 6 LLMs and 4 prompting strategies. Source of M-IRT's negation and contrapositive MRs.

[11] [Language Models Are Greedy Reasoners: A Systematic Formal Analysis of Chain-of-Thought (PrOntoQA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01240) — ICLR 2023. PCFG over a typed ontology that generates reasoning questions with derivable ground truth. Four-step generation: ontology → proof → NL context → query/CoT/label. Three control variables (depth, ontology type, predicate arity). The closest precedent for M-IRT's MIC generator for propositional reasoning.

[12] [Training Verifiers to Solve Math Word Problems (GSM8K)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168) — 8.5K grade-school math word problems, 2–8 elementary-arithmetic steps. Metamorphic-friendly: distractor invariance, negation of quantities. One of M-IRT's arithmetic-cluster generators.

[13] [MAWPS: A Math Word Problem Repository](https://aclanthology.org/N16-1136/) — NAACL 2016. Online repository of math word problems, paired with GSM8K as the arithmetic-cluster generator template.

[14] [LogicBench: Towards Systematic Evaluation of Logical Reasoning Ability of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15522) — ACL 2024. 25 reasoning patterns across propositional, first-order, non-monotonic logics. Shows existing LLMs struggle with negations and complex reasoning. 3-stage generation (GPT-3.5 ontology → context+question → conclusion). Source of M-IRT's syllogistic sub-generator and explicit negation sensitivity.

[15] [FOLIO: Natural Language Reasoning with First-Order Logic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00840) — EMNLP 2024. 1,430 examples paired with one of 487 sets of premises, FOL annotations auto-verified by an FOL inference engine. The only standard benchmark where logical correctness is machine-checked, providing an external validation oracle for M-IRT (used on a held-out slice, never trained on).

[16] [Metamorphic Testing: A Review of Challenges and Opportunities](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3143561) — ACM Computing Surveys 2018. Defines MR as R_i(input) ⇒ R_o(output). The field-level survey grounding the formal definition of M-IRT's metamorphic relations.

[17] [Metamorphic Testing of Large Language Models for Natural Language Processing (LLMORPH)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02108) — ICSME 2025. Catalogues 191 NLP MRs across 24 tasks; runs 561,267 test executions on GPT-4, Llama-3, Hermes-2; ~60% true-positive rate; MRs 9/142/154 flagged as high-failure-low-FP. 11% of failures missed by traditional testing. Source of M-IRT's paraphrase and distractor MRs.

## Follow-up Questions

- Does girth (Python) support Samejima GRM with Bock-Aitkin EM out of the box, or do we need mirt via rpy2? Choi et al. [6] use PyMC3 with NUTS sampling — should M-IRT switch to full Bayesian estimation given n_models <= 10, where MML asymptotic assumptions break down?
- Should MICs be generated once per session (cached) or fresh per model to harden against memorization, and how does each choice affect the contamination-simulation protocol?
- Is 41 Gauss-Hermite quadrature nodes sufficient for n_models < 10 (small-marginal-MLE concern), or should we increase to 61 nodes / switch to adaptive quadrature?
- How to handle models that produce refusals or non-parsable outputs in consistency scoring — should refusals be scored as Grade 0 (inconsistent) or excluded from the cluster grade entirely?
- What is the right negative control for M-IRT — a random / shuffled-label baseline that should score approximately 0 on theta_M-IRT, plus an 'impossible premise' cluster that should detect surface-pattern matching?
- How do we verify that M-IRT's theta_M-IRT actually correlates with static-benchmark accuracy at r >= 0.85 when contamination is absent, before the contamination-simulation experiment? — this is the 'positive control' that the experiment needs to clear first.
- Roberts et al.'s 'Compute Trends Across Three Years of Foundation Model Training' (referenced in the artifact plan) was not located within the search budget; do we adopt the [7] survey's taxonomy of matching-based vs comparison-based detection as a substitute, and is that defensible to a Percy Liang-style reviewer?
