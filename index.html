M-IRT: Reference-Free LLM Evaluation via Samejima Graded Response Models on Dynamically Generated Metamorphic Item Clusters

Anonymous Authors

Static benchmarks with frozen answer keys are no longer trustworthy, since the training corpora of modern language models have absorbed a large share of the public web. This paper asks whether reasoning capability can be measured when no answer key is trusted to be free of contamination, and answers yes: across five public LLMs and one hundred dynamically generated Metamorphic Item Clusters, the GRM-estimated latent ability θ correlates with MMLU at Pearson r = 0.975 (Spearman ρ = 0.90, Kendall τ = 0.80), with no fixed answer key in the loop. The full pipeline runs in three minutes for under $0.05.

Contributions

What this paper adds

Framework

Reference-free M-IRT

A scoring framework that produces Metamorphic Item Clusters dynamically per session, derives ordinal coherence grades from relational invariants, and fits a Samejima Graded Response Model to recover a per-model latent ability θ with no fixed answer key in the loop.

Pipeline

Reproducible at $0.05

One hundred MICs spanning propositional syllogisms and grade-school arithmetic, queried on five public LLMs, scored and fitted end-to-end in three minutes for under $0.05. The ablation against three binary consistency baselines on the same clusters shows θ (Pearson r = 0.975) exceeds every one of them.

Test & protocol

Stress test plus a clearly scoped next step

An artificial stress test on Qwen 2.5 7B confirms coherence grading penalises internal inconsistency (Δseed = +0.07, Δθ = −1.38). A natural-contamination protocol (system-prompt injection plus re-query) is laid out as the experiment the framework's hypothesis demands.

Method

How M-IRT scores a model without an answer key

Each cluster carries four variants — seed, paraphrase, negation, and inverse / contrapositive — bound by a relational invariant whose oracle is derivable from the question text alone. No absolute label exists. A model is scored by how many of the four variants it answers coherently; that count is mapped to a 3-point ordinal grade (0 = near-random, 1 = partially coherent, 2 = highly coherent) and a Samejima Graded Response Model fits a per-model latent ability θ from the response matrix.

  1. Generate the clusters

    Twelve logic templates and twelve arithmetic templates emit fifty propositional-syllogism clusters and fifty grade-school word-problem clusters, one hundred in total. Each cluster carries a relational invariant table whose oracle is derivable from the question text alone, so the contamination surface is the single session that ran it.

    Paraphrases use a noun pool disjoint from the seed so the seed and the paraphrase never share vocabulary. The negation variant's oracle is the Boolean complement (logic) or the fixed label "NO" (arithmetic); the inverse variant's oracle is a quantity that appears explicitly in the seed's question text.

  2. Query the models

    Five public LLMs (GPT-4o-mini, Claude Haiku 4.5, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct, Mistral Small 24B Instruct 2501) receive each of the four hundred variant questions in a single zero-shot prompt. All two thousand calls complete in 167 s at $0.048 cumulative spend, with the same prompt template across models and no worked examples.

  3. Score ordinal coherence

    For each (model, cluster) pair, count the variants whose response matches the role-appropriate oracle and map that count to a 3-point ordinal grade. The scoring is deterministic and parser-level, with no separate judge model or human rater. Mapping the raw count 0–4 into three ordered bins is the smallest K that separates "near-random" from "partially coherent" from "highly coherent" and is the choice the present panel of five models and one hundred items can stably fit.

  4. Fit a GRM and validate

    Fit the Samejima Graded Response Model by marginal maximum likelihood on the (5 × 100) response matrix, recovering per-model θ, per-item discrimination α, and ordered difficulty thresholds βi,k. Two validation sub-experiments check the result: an external correlation against MMLU and HELM, and an artificial stress test that forces internal inconsistency on thirty percent of clusters.

Stress test on Qwen 2.5 7B

On thirty percent of clusters the seed response is forced to the oracle while the metamorphic variants stay untouched. Seed-only accuracy rises by +0.07 on average; the GRM-estimated θ falls by an average of −1.382 with standard deviation 0.007 across three random seeds. The sign flip on θ is the contamination- resistance signal at the response-matrix level: forcing the seed right while the metamorphic slots stay wrong produces a profile the GRM penalises.

Limitations

Where the present evidence falls short

  • Five-model panel

    The bootstrap CI on Pearson r spans [0.59, 1.00]; the lower bound already allows r = 0.59, well below the r > 0.85 hypothesis criterion. The hypothesis specifies n ≥ 15; the present artifact runs at n = 5 because the OpenRouter cost ceiling restricted the panel.

  • Two reasoning domains

    MICs cover propositional syllogisms and grade-school arithmetic. Real reasoning also includes multi-hop reading comprehension and first-order logic (FOLIO). The reported MMLU correlation therefore reflects the syllogistic-plus-arithmetic component of reasoning capability, not MMLU's history, law, medicine, or biology domains directly.

  • Artificial stress test, not natural contamination

    The response-matrix-level stress test confirms that coherence grading penalises an internally inconsistent profile, but does not test the contamination-resistance claim in the realistic sense. A natural-contamination experiment (inject the seed oracle into the system prompt, re-query all four variants, measure whether coherence rises on the metamorphic slots) is the proper test and is listed as future work.

  • Twelve templates per domain

    Twelve logic templates and twelve arithmetic templates cover the classical syllogistic forms and the most common word-problem operations, but are not exhaustive. A larger generator bank would yield finer per-item discrimination estimates.

  • Polytomous response scale

    The 0/1/2 mapping compresses 5 raw counts (0–4) into 3 categories. With a larger panel, K = 4 or K = 5 would let the GRM exploit more granularity at the cost of greater estimation variance.

  • Reference-free for what?

    A model that memorises the form of the metamorphic relation (e.g., that "not P" flips a V/I label) would still answer all four variants coherently and would receive a high θ. That is the correct outcome (the model has internalised a logical rule) but it is not a contamination-resistance result. The natural-contamination protocol is the test that distinguishes "internalised the rule" from "memorised the seeds".
